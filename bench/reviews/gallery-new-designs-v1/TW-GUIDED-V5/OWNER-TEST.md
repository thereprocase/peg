# Curved-guide physical check

Test the curved-roof prototype with the taller fins. See [NOTES.md](NOTES.md)
for the complete return and pickup motions. The CAD and stiffness checks describe
the reference geometry; natural handling and printed behavior need observation.

- Fit the printed holder to the actual board and check the hooks, hoops and
  frame for movement before loading it. Record board movement separately from
  frame flex. Dry-fit the four taller fins, keep adhesive off their catching
  faces and let the foot bonds cure before testing.
- Try each actual tweezer in every station, then load all four. Confirm the
  retained seated angles and tip clearance, including the longest pair.
  Check the lower stations at their new 28 mm spacing.
- From the normal bench position, present a tool above and to your left, then
  sweep it right. Observe where the curved roof first meets the stronger arm
  section and whether it guides the tool down around the fin. Align above
  the taller fin, then allow the final free downward settle as described in
  NOTES. The fragile tips should stay clear.
- Repeat ordinary returns by feel, including slightly high and lateral starts.
  Record misses, a tool perched on a fin or guide, and returns that need an
  extra grip or a precision correction. Compare against the frozen taller-fin
  holder without the guide when practical.
- Pick up each tool independently with the three neighbors stored. Lift
  initially, then sweep continuously up-left and outward along the selected
  complete escape route, then try the usual natural pickup. Check for
  roof hooking, contact with a neighboring tool, and adequate finger access.
- Check that every guide has a smooth reachable surface after support removal.
  Record any rough contact, thin join damage, tool scratching or glue movement.
  Apply the same ordinary bench bump used for the earlier holder and note
  whether a fully seated tool lifts or perches on the taller fin.

Keep observations attached to the tested body, fin and tool versions. A clear
sampled path is not a measured capture rate, and the solid fixed-peg calculation
does not measure a printed guide, adhesive bond or pegboard retention.
