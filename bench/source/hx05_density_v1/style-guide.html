REPRO / WORKSHOP PARTS · PROPOSED DESIGN LANGUAGE v1

Rounded contact. Tapered support. Deliberate space.

Start with the tool and the print pose. Give each contact its own clear form, merge its load paths into the back body, and let the empty regions echo those forms. Repeated proportions make the family coherent; the printer sets the growth rules.

01 / Shape in print Z

Control growth; allow contraction.

Use shallow outward chamfers and tapered feet. Match the top and bottom chamfers when symmetry improves the form; the contracting side can be steeper when function calls for it.

A round-over crossing Z can grow too fast near its start. Draw the layer sections first. For a hole, narrowing the air region means growing material: the same limit applies to its roof.

02 / Finish freely in XY

Use a small, repeated edge hierarchy.

R6 / R2 / C0.5 mm are starting sizes: primary silhouette, secondary junction, edge break. Scale them to the part. Fillets soften contact and major outlines; chamfers clarify planes and cut entrances.

XY sections have no overhang-angle limit. Their changes between successive Z layers still obey the growth rule. These sizes never override a tested fit or an exact interface.

03 / Give the mass a job

Contact: cylinders, capsules and tested sockets.

Transfer: wedges and ribbons with broad roots.

Support: feet grown into the holder bottoms and back body.

Air: windows that echo the outer contour, with consistent edge margins.

One primary form, a few clear transitions.

Keep back bodies quiet. Merge local supports into the main structure. Extra cheeks, islands and flanges need a functional reason. Use repeated angles and tangent joins instead of a collection of unrelated blobs.

04 / Structure and density

Put density where forces transfer.

Use continuous sleeve skins, root fan-outs and upper/lower load paths. Keep them broad in printed XY. Sparse bulk occupies the remaining envelope; 100% infill modifiers can have arbitrary shapes and stick out as selection handles.

Modifiers change the overlap; they do not become printed bumps. Gravity, extraction and twisting remain separate load cases. XY force direction alone does not prove low interlayer stress.

05 / Preserve functional geometry

Tested socket flats, stops, engagement, clocking, lead-ins and vents have their own specification. Peg bearing arcs and board-facing seams have their own specification too. Apply the design language to the surrounding body.

Give an intentional exception a name: a tested bore roof, a bridge, a peg or a support interface. Review it in its actual print pose. Avoid quietly relaxing the 30° rule across an entire part.

Fit is exact; surrounding form is editable.

06 / Make the family recognizable

Repeat the radius hierarchy, tapered root shape, window language and entrance treatment. Favor bilateral visual balance where the tool layout permits it. Show a small number of readable planes.

inch metric Torx — CAD variants; filament color is independent.

Use recessed markings on top-facing pads. Keep identification clear and secondary to the working form.

One visual grammar, several functional shapes.

Release review: inspect XY layer silhouettes → check positive material growth in print Z → verify unchanged interfaces and access → slice the actual material/profile with density modifiers → review supports, bridges and removal. Rounded outlines and genetic ranking are separate from strength qualification.
